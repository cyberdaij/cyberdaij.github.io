Welcome

I'm an Associate Professor in the Department of Computer Science at Worcester Polytechnic Institute (WPI). My research lies at the intersection of artificial intelligence, cybersecurity, and networked and distributed systems, with a particular focus on the security and safety of large language models (LLMs) and autonomous AI agents. My research spans LLM and agent security, model privacy and extraction, retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) security, vulnerability analysis, attack detection, secure coding, and cybersecurity education. I have published in leading security, AI, systems, and data management venues, including NDSS, ICML, ACL, EMNLP, SenSys, SIGMOD, SIGCSE, IEEE TDSC, and IEEE TIFS. My research has been supported primarily by the National Science Foundation (NSF) and other federal agencies. I currently serve as an Associate Editor for IEEE TDSC and as an Artifact Evaluation Co-Chair for ACM CCS 2026.

I earned my PhD in Information Sciences and Technology with a specialization in cybersecurity from The Pennsylvania State University (PSU) in 2014, under the mentorship of Prof. Peng Liu. Prior to that, I earned my BS degree in Information Security in 2007 and my MS degree in Network Control in 2010, both from The University of Science and Technology of China (USTC).

I'm also actively involved in cybersecurity education and workforce development. I serve on the College Board’s Advanced Placement® (AP) Networking Assessment Committee and have contributed to the development of AP Networking Credential and AP Cybersecurity. I have also participated in the ACM CSEC Foundational Content Update effort and lead and contribute to federally funded initiatives that expand hands-on AI and cybersecurity education and workforce training for students, educators, and professionals.
